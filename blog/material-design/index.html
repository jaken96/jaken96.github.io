Making the Web Material

Google introduced Material Design in 2014 with a simple question: what if software behaved like something you could touch? Twelve years later, the paper has learned to stretch, spring, change color, and—apparently—develop a personality.

I have been using Google products for most of my life. Search, Gmail, Android, Maps—if Google made a smart refrigerator tomorrow, there is a non-zero chance I would hand it my grocery list.

Despite this, I had never stopped to ask why all of those products feel related. They do not look identical, but they behave like cousins at a family reunion. They use the same gestures. They arrange information in familiar layers. They all seem to agree that a button should look very button-y.

The answer is Material Design: a shared design system made up of guidance, components, and tools. In this post, I will explore how it changed over time. More importantly, I will attempt to build the entire post using the latest version of the system. We are going full Material.

01 · ORIGIN STORY

The paper years

Material Design debuted at Google I/O in 2014. Internally, it had been called “Quantum Paper,” which is both an excellent project name and a phrase I would expect to hear right before someone opens a portal.

The paper metaphor gave early Material a set of rules. Surfaces could stack, cast shadows, and move—but they could not pass through one another or ignore physics whenever it was convenient. A floating action button sat above the page because, visually, it was a separate piece of material.

Material 2 arrived in 2018 and expanded the component system. It also made more room for brands to customize the base layer. Think of it as Google handing everyone the same box of LEGO bricks, then politely asking us not to glue a wheel to a sandwich.

02 · PERSONALIZATION

Material becomes you

Material 3 launched in 2021 with Material You. Instead of choosing a static brand palette and calling it a day, Android could generate a coordinated color scheme from your wallpaper. Your phone still followed the rules, but the rules finally included you.

This page uses the same basic idea. Pick a seed color below and the surfaces, controls, outlines, and text colors update as a family. No individual color is working alone.

03 · EMOTION, ON PURPOSE

Then things got Expressive

Material 3 Expressive was announced in 2025 and continues to be Google’s current Material direction. Google expanded the system again at I/O 2026 with more adaptive components and a customizable motion-physics system. It builds on Material You with bolder type, contrasting shapes, vibrant color, and springy motion. The goal is not decoration for decoration’s sake. Size, shape, and containment tell your eyes what matters first.

05 · CONCLUSION

It is still paper. Kind of.

Material Design started by giving software a physical metaphor. Paper established where things lived, shadows explained which surface was on top, and motion showed how one state became another.

The latest system keeps that clarity, but loosens the tie. Material can now be pill-shaped, flower-shaped, loud, calm, springy, or personalized from a single color. The important part is not the exact corner radius. It is that every visual choice helps communicate what the interface wants you to notice or do.

Did this article need a wavy progress indicator? No. Did I enjoy making one? Absolutely. Expressive can still be functional, but functional is finally allowed to have a little fun.
